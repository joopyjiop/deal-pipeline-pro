# Deal Pipeline Pro — Minimalist MVP Plan

Status: proposed operating plan for the first customer cohort.

This plan applies the business skills in `skills/minimalist-entrepreneur/` to the existing source-first, owner-controlled pipeline.

## One-sentence product promise

Deal Pipeline Pro helps one wholesaler turn a public real-estate source into a reviewable evidence record and a qualified buyer conversation without fabricated PII.

## The smallest valuable workflow

1. **Capture one public source** — an owner-provided county, Auction.com, assessor, recorder, or other permitted public URL.
2. **Preserve evidence** — retain the source URL, reference, date, quoted evidence, and capture status.
3. **Review manually** — the owner decides whether the record is real, relevant, and complete.
4. **Approve one lead** — only verified, non-fabricated records enter the surfaced lead workspace.
5. **Match one buyer** — only approved buyers and approved leads can enter the match board.

The crawler is a research assistant, not an autonomous lead approver. A crawl may produce evidence; it does not create permission to contact a person or claim a deal is viable.

## Manual-first operating process

### Trigger

The owner finds a public source or receives a source URL from a trusted workflow.

### Magic piece of paper

1. Paste one source URL into Toolkit.
2. Capture a bounded page or link batch.
3. Record the source date and reference.
4. Review the evidence snapshot and the official page.
5. Reject pages that are blocked, incomplete, duplicated, or not clearly relevant.
6. Create or qualify a candidate only when the address, location, reference, date, and distress evidence are explicit.
7. Run the optional consultant review as a recommendation only.
8. Approve the lead manually.
9. Match it to an approved buyer and record the fit.
10. Contact people only through lawful, permission-aware channels after the owner confirms the record.

### Definition of done for one lead

- Source URL opens successfully.
- Source reference and source date are recorded.
- Property location is explicit in the source.
- Distress evidence is quoted or summarized faithfully.
- No fabricated name, phone, email, or address was created.
- Owner reviewed and approved the record.
- Any buyer match has a written fit summary.

## Customer validation

### Initial community hypothesis

- Independent wholesalers and small real-estate operators who already spend time reviewing foreclosure, tax-sale, assessor, recorder, and auction records.
- Local investor groups and REIA communities where members can show how they currently research and qualify deals.
- Active buyers who need a cleaner, evidence-backed deal handoff rather than another unverified lead list.

### Validation target before broadening scope

- Interview 10 real operators or buyers.
- Deliver the manual workflow to 3 of them.
- Get at least 3 people to pay for a bounded result or commit to a paid pilot.
- Track whether the same evidence fields and review steps repeat across customers.
- Do not build broader crawling, scoring, or automation until the manual workflow is repeatedly useful.

### Questions to ask

- What source do you check first, and what makes it trustworthy?
- How much time does one usable lead take today?
- What causes you to reject a candidate?
- What evidence would you need before contacting a buyer or seller?
- Would you pay for a reviewed, source-linked record? At what price?

## Initial pricing experiment

Use a reversible founding-pilot test rather than a permanent pricing architecture:

- **Founding owner pilot:** $49/month for one owner, bounded source batches, evidence review workspace, and buyer matching.
- **Concierge alternative:** $99 per manually reviewed batch for customers who do not want a recurring commitment.
- **No unlimited crawling promise:** usage is bounded by source permissions, hosting capacity, and review quality.
- Revisit pricing after 3 paying pilots and a clear measure of time saved or qualified buyer conversations created.

These are hypotheses to test, not market claims. Keep a simple ledger of revenue, browser/API costs, review time, and gross margin.

## Marketing and first customers

Start with one channel: direct, personal outreach in communities the owner genuinely belongs to.

1. Share the problem honestly: invented lead data wastes calling time and creates compliance risk.
2. Demonstrate one source-to-review workflow using public evidence.
3. Invite 10 operators to a short interview, not a mass signup funnel.
4. Offer the founding pilot to people who have already described the problem.
5. Publish one useful weekly artifact: a source-review checklist, evidence standard, or anonymized workflow lesson.
6. Build an email list only from people who explicitly opt in.

Do not use scraped contact lists, fabricated testimonials, unsolicited dialing, or claims that a record is verified when it has not passed owner review.

## Sustainable growth rules

- Keep Camofox/Firecrawl usage bounded and observable.
- Prefer manual review over expensive automation until customers repeatedly ask for the same step.
- Keep browser hosting and API costs below pilot revenue.
- Treat paid acquisition, large VM upgrades, and broad site crawling as reversible experiments.
- Hire only after the owner has a stable review process and the bottleneck is clearly delivery.
- Keep the owner gate, fabricated-row exclusion, and evidence requirements permanently in the product path.

## Values applied to the product

1. **No source, no surface.** A lead without evidence is not a lead.
2. **Review beats theater.** AI can summarize evidence, but the owner makes the decision.
3. **Useful before broad.** A small batch that saves real time beats an impressive crawl that creates noise.
4. **Permission-aware growth.** Buyer and seller contact paths must respect authorization and applicable law.
5. **Profit funds independence.** Expand only when the workflow saves measurable time or produces paid value.

## 30-day experiment

### Week 1 — processize

- Run five manual source reviews.
- Record time per source, rejection reasons, and missing fields.
- Fix only the most repeated friction.

### Week 2 — community

- Interview 10 operators/buyers.
- Show the evidence chain, not a giant feature list.
- Select three pilot users.

### Week 3 — sell and deliver

- Charge for the pilot.
- Deliver bounded batches manually with owner review.
- Ask each pilot what they would repeat next week.

### Week 4 — decide

- Continue if at least three users paid or committed and the workflow is repeatably useful.
- Simplify if users value only one part of the flow.
- Pause new automation if the browser or source quality is the bottleneck.

## Current product boundary

The current app should optimize for: **public source → evidence snapshot → owner-approved lead → approved buyer match**.

It should not yet promise: unlimited website coverage, autonomous verification, fabricated skip-trace data, automatic seller outreach, or fully unattended deal-making.
